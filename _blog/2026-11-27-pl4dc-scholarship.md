Design Computation (DC) is an approach in architecture, 
engineering, and construction  that leverages computation to
generate building designs, manage the associated data,
and evaluate design decisions <a class="cite" id="cite-1" href="#ref-1">[1]</a>.
DC differs to CAD-based design modelling, where the designer uses
interactive geometry applications to manually create the finished building design. With this form of direct modelling there are no opportunities
 to record the rationale for the design and any subsequent design exploration requires further arduous manual edits. 
  In Design Computation however, programs are written to generate the designs themselves as their output, 
 explicitly programming the logic and method of its realisation, 
 and enabling design intent to be more easily captured. The modification and re-execution of the program allows the designer to efficiently 
 explore a larger
 solution space of alternatives or other forms of optimisation.

At the heart of DC is programming, which must then be expressed in some programming language or languages. Both textual programming languages and visual programming  languages are widely deployed in this space; some of these are general purpose and others are specialised, used only in these domains.

Following some long discussions with experts in industry (Al Fisher
from Buro Happold) and academia (Prof Robert Aish, Bartlett School of Architecture, UCL)
about how programming languages for design computation could be advanced, we put
together a position paper on our ideas, published at Onward! 2024 <a class="cite" id="cite-2" href="#ref-2">[2]</a>.

Happily, our ongoing conversations have led to the generous support 
from Buro Happold for a 3.5 year PhD scholarship at the University of Kent
to work with me on these ideas, collaborating with Al and others at Buro Happold.

This PhD project responds to the challenge: how can the unique ways of collaborative thinking and reasoning, which characterise the 
multi-disciplinary design of physical architecture and building engineering, be supported by new forms of programming languages, programming systems, and programming tools?
This is an opportunity for some original language research; the potential exists for the ideas and methods developed in this specific domain to evolve into more general-purpose programming paradigms (following in the path of important historical 
precedents).

Funding is available for both international and home students,
and includes travel funding. 
I have more [details available here](https://plas4sci.github.io/pl4dc-phd),
and details of how to apply, along with the requirements for a candidate can be found [here](https://www.kent.ac.uk/scholarships/search/FNADPLDCOM01).

The deadline for applications is December 22<sup>nd</sup> 2026 with the
aim of the candidate starting in April 2027. Please do share with any
potential candidates who might be interested.
